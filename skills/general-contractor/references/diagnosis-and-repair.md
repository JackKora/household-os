# Diagnosis and repair

Use this reference for a physical symptom, a repair choice, or a question about a material or product. Start with the smallest safe fact-gathering step that could meaningfully change the recommendation.

## Separate what is seen from why it happened

Name the symptom without treating it as the cause. A stain, crack, odor, condensation, tripped breaker, or uneven floor can have several causes, including conditions outside the visible area. Rank plausible causes by the available evidence, explain what would distinguish them, and state the confidence level.

Prefer non-destructive observation first: note location and extent; compare timing with rain, temperature, equipment operation, or loading; take dated photos; check accessible areas with light and simple measurement; and review relevant maintenance or work history. Escalate to a targeted opening, moisture or electrical measurement, camera inspection, pressure test, or qualified specialist only when it would resolve a consequential uncertainty. Do not advise tests that expose people to electrical, gas, fall, structural, contamination, or other high-consequence hazards.

Follow the path of water, air, heat, loads, and utilities through the assembly. A finish failure can originate at flashing, drainage, a roof penetration, a plumbing route, an air leak, insufficient ventilation, settlement, or an altered load path. Avoid repairing the visible endpoint until the cause is controlled and the affected materials can dry, be cleaned, or be assessed.

## Choose the repair deliberately

Describe the decision as one of these, as applicable:

- **Emergency stabilization:** limits immediate danger or damage while the right responder takes over.
- **Temporary mitigation:** reduces exposure or deterioration while diagnosis, design, permits, materials, or scheduling proceed; say what it does not solve and when it must be revisited.
- **Durable repair:** addresses the verified failure mechanism, restores compatible layers and details, and allows appropriate inspection or testing before closure.
- **Replacement or redesign:** is preferable when the component is near end of life, repeatedly fails, cannot be repaired compatibly, has broad concealed damage, or a repair would cost disproportionately relative to remaining service.

Compare options by root-cause control, compatibility with the existing assembly, durability, maintenance, serviceability, disruption, reversibility, cost, and likelihood of revealing hidden conditions. Do not substitute a surface treatment for drainage, flashing, ventilation, structural, plumbing, or electrical correction when that correction is indicated.

## Account for concealed conditions and products

State what remains unknown behind finishes, below grade, or inside equipment. Include a decision point: proceed with an allowance or contingency, make a limited investigative opening, obtain a specialist evaluation, or defer work. Explain the trigger for revising scope rather than presenting an uncertain condition as settled.

For a material, system, or repair method, research current primary technical sources when the choice is material. Use the manufacturer’s current installation instructions, technical data sheet, compatibility guidance, and warranty terms for the exact product and assembly; verify that adjacent materials and fasteners are permitted together. For safety, performance, or hazard questions, favor current government, utility, standards, or manufacturer sources and distinguish documented requirements from trade convention or marketing claims. Cite document version and access date when they could affect the decision.
