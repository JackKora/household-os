# Projects and contractors

Use this reference to plan or review a rehab, remodel, addition, major replacement, bid, contractor work, or closeout. Turn the desired outcome into an observable, comparable scope before choosing a price or schedule.

## Define scope and uncertainty

Record existing conditions, intended performance and appearance, inclusions, exclusions, drawings or engineering needed, owner-supplied items, access and protection, demolition and disposal, allowances, inspection responsibilities, testing or commissioning, cleanup, closeout documents, and acceptance criteria. Treat incomplete design, concealed conditions, long-lead selections, and jurisdictional review as uncertainty—not as fixed price or schedule commitments.

Give estimates as dated planning ranges, not quotes. Name the location, scope, assumptions, exclusions, price basis, and uncertainty. Include permits, design, labor, materials, equipment, disposal, temporary protection, and a contingency proportionate to the design maturity and hidden-condition risk. Do not use a national unit-cost average as a local bid proxy.

## Sequence work around dependencies

Start with investigation, design, hazard control, permits, procurement, and site protection. Then adapt the usual dependency order to the actual assembly: drainage and structural work; roof and water management; enclosure; rough plumbing, electrical, and HVAC; required inspections; insulation and air sealing; finishes; equipment startup or commissioning; and punch-list closeout. Make dependencies explicit before setting the schedule. A choice that blocks inspection, access, drying, flashing continuity, or future service should be reconsidered.

## Research project-specific requirements

For permits, inspections, codes, licensing, zoning, or amendments, verify the current requirement with the relevant authority having jurisdiction. Identify the local code edition, effective date, amendments, and permit or inspection process rather than assuming the model code applies. For specified products and assemblies, verify the current manufacturer documents and required installation sequence. Cite the source, version or effective date, and access date for consequential claims. Only the relevant authority or qualified professional can approve work or certify compliance.

## Compare bids and review work

Normalize bids before comparing price: align quantities, product or performance specifications, allowances, exclusions, permits, demolition and disposal, schedule, site protection, cleanup, warranty, payment milestones, substitutions, and change-order process. Ask about gaps or vague items before treating a lower number as savings. Verify required license and insurance information through the applicable official sources; do not represent that verification as a personal credential or approval.

During work, compare observable results with the approved scope, plans, manufacturer requirements, and required inspections. Document conditions with dates, photos, and focused questions while access remains available. Create a punch list that states the location, unmet requirement, requested correction, supporting document or observation, and completion check. Close out with final inspections as applicable, commissioning or test records, warranties, product data, manuals, lien releases or other contractual documents when appropriate, cleanup, and confirmation that accepted punch-list items are complete. Do not certify workmanship, code compliance, completion, or payment entitlement from remote or incomplete evidence.
