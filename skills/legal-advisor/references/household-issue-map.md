# Household Legal Issue Map

Use this reference to spot the material legal dimensions of a personal or household question. It is a routing checklist, not a catalog of current law. Consider only the sections connected to the user's actual request.

## Estate, incapacity, and probate

- wills, trusts, intestacy, probate, and estate administration
- financial powers of attorney, healthcare powers, living wills, advance directives, and surrogate decision-making
- guardianship, conservatorship, and nominations for minor children
- beneficiary designations, payable- or transfer-on-death arrangements, jointly owned property, deeds, and asset titling
- fiduciary duties, incapacity, elder exploitation, long-term care, digital assets, and funeral instructions

Do not treat a will as the entire estate plan. Check non-probate transfers, ownership, beneficiary designations, execution formalities, revocation, acceptance by institutions, and agency-specific authority. Never assume an ordinary power of attorney satisfies a government program's separate appointment process.

## Family and domestic relations

- marriage, domestic partnership, and prenuptial or postnuptial agreements
- separation, divorce, annulment, property division, and support
- parentage, adoption, guardianship, custody, parenting time, relocation, and interstate proceedings
- domestic violence, protective orders, name changes, vital records, assisted reproduction, and surrogacy

Do not assume the interests of partners, parents, children, or other family members are aligned. Use `parenting` for child development and caregiver response; use this skill for legal rights, duties, documents, standards, and procedure.

## Housing and real property

For renting or leasing, consider lease formation and renewal, rent and fees, security deposits, utilities, repairs and habitability, entry and privacy, roommates and subletting, pets and assistance animals, accommodations, notices, termination, eviction, retaliation, and fair housing. Identify whether the user is acting as tenant, landlord, property manager, guarantor, or occupant.

For ownership, consider purchase and sale agreements, disclosures, inspections, financing and escrow, title and surveys, deeds and recording, liens, foreclosure, co-ownership and partition, easements and boundaries, nuisance, HOA or condominium rules, zoning, permits, short-term rentals, property taxes, construction contracts, defects, warranties, and mechanic's liens.

Use `financial-advisor` for affordability, return, financing tradeoffs, tax modeling, and insurance amounts. Use this skill for legal rights, documents, title, liability, enforcement, and procedure.

## Contracts, consumer, debt, and insurance

- formation, scope, defined terms, performance, warranties, default, remedies, releases, guarantees, arbitration, and electronic signatures
- home services, contractors, moving and storage, vehicles, subscriptions, warranties, scams, and unfair or deceptive practices
- credit reporting, loans, debt collection, repossession, foreclosure, garnishment, and bankruptcy triage
- home, renters, auto, umbrella, health, disability, and life coverage; claims, exclusions, denials, appeals, cooperation, subrogation, and settlement releases

Use `financial-advisor` for how much coverage to buy or the financial effect of debt. Use this skill for coverage interpretation, claims, collections, legal exposure, and enforcement.

## Employment, benefits, education, and caregiving

- offer letters, compensation, classification, confidentiality, restrictive covenants, wages, leave, accommodations, discrimination, retaliation, termination, severance, and unemployment
- Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, disability, veterans' benefits, pensions, representative payees, agency denials, hearings, and appeals
- special education, accommodations, discipline, student records, discrimination, enrollment, and childcare agreements
- medical consent, access to records, privacy, caregiver authority, long-term-care agreements, and disability access

Use `wellness-coach` for health and treatment questions and `parenting` for child development or family response. Use this skill for consent, records, accommodations, benefits, contracts, and legal process.

## Civil disputes, personal rights, and everyday administration

- personal injury, property damage, negligence, nuisance, trespass, defamation, neighbor disputes, small claims, mediation, arbitration, settlement, judgments, and collections
- identity theft, data breaches, account access, recordings, surveillance, online terms, copyright, trademarks, and digital assets
- government records, licenses, name changes, notarization, authentication, and vital records
- household employees, caregivers, independent contractors, modest side businesses, permits, client or vendor agreements, and ordinary intellectual-property ownership

## Triage-first areas

For criminal investigations or charges, immigration status or removal exposure, domestic violence, emergency protective orders, contested custody or child removal, active litigation or appeals, subpoenas or injunctions, bankruptcy filings, complex tax-driven estate planning, and large irreversible transactions, provide a concise orientation and deadline-preserving next steps before deeper analysis. Explain what type of qualified help fits and why.

Complex corporate, securities, antitrust, patent-prosecution, and commercial matters are outside ordinary household scope. Recognize and route them rather than trying to build a comprehensive specialty practice inside this skill.
