# Planning and Document Review

Use this reference for legal planning and for reviewing wills, trusts, powers of attorney, advance directives, leases, deeds, insurance policies, employment terms, releases, contracts, and similar documents.

## Identify the requested level

Distinguish among:

- plain-language explanation
- issue and risk review
- comparison with the user's objectives
- negotiation suggestions
- drafting assistance
- execution, recording, delivery, filing, or submission guidance

Do not turn a request for a quick explanation into exhaustive drafting analysis. Conversely, do not describe a draft as ready to sign when legal validity depends on jurisdiction-specific language or formalities.

## Confirm the operative material

Before giving a definitive document conclusion, determine whether the user provided the complete operative version, including:

- amendments, riders, schedules, exhibits, addenda, and incorporated policies
- definitions and external terms referenced by link or title
- signatures, dates, initials, acknowledgments, witnesses, and notarization
- related documents that alter priority, ownership, beneficiaries, coverage, or remedies

Flag missing pages, unreadable text, inconsistent versions, OCR uncertainty, and unsigned or undated material. Preserve originals and metadata when a dispute may arise; work from a copy.

## Review the legal mechanics

Consider only the dimensions material to the document:

- parties, roles, authority, capacity, and effective date
- definitions, scope, obligations, conditions, discretion, and standards of performance
- money, adjustment mechanisms, taxes, expenses, deposits, and reimbursements
- notices, deadlines, renewal, automatic renewal, cancellation, and termination
- default, cure, remedies, specific performance, liquidated damages, and equitable relief
- representations, warranties, disclaimers, indemnity, insurance, and liability limits
- confidentiality, privacy, data use, publicity, ownership, and intellectual property
- assignment, delegation, successors, beneficiaries, integration, amendment, severability, and waiver
- governing law, venue, arbitration, jury waiver, class waiver, attorney fees, and costs
- signatures, witnesses, notarization, delivery, acceptance, recording, and other formalities

Translate the important effect into ordinary language. Identify unusual one-sided provisions, internal conflicts, missing protections, operational burdens, and the term most likely to surprise the user.

## Estate and incapacity planning

Do not treat a will as the entire plan. Reconcile the user's objectives with ownership, beneficiary designations, payable- or transfer-on-death arrangements, jointly held property, trusts, deeds, insurance, retirement accounts, digital assets, guardianship nominations, and probate administration.

Distinguish financial power of attorney, healthcare power or proxy, living will or advance directive, guardianship or conservatorship, and agency-specific authority. Check durability, when authority begins, scope, successor agents, gifting and self-dealing powers, revocation, acceptance, execution formalities, and the effect of moving jurisdictions. An ordinary power of attorney may not satisfy a federal agency's separate appointment process.

For execution-sensitive instruments, prefer a current official statutory form when it fits the user's objective. Explain where a customized lawyer-drafted instrument may be safer, especially for blended families, adverse interests, special-needs planning, tax planning, business ownership, property in multiple jurisdictions, or likely contests.

## Housing and property documents

For leases, identify the user's role and review premises, term, renewal, rent, fees, utilities, deposits, repairs, entry, alterations, occupancy, guests, subletting, pets and assistance animals, insurance, casualty, default, notice, termination, surrender, remedies, and local required disclosures.

For purchases, sales, deeds, or ownership arrangements, review contingencies, disclosures, inspection, financing, title, survey, easements, prorations, risk of loss, closing, possession, warranties, co-ownership, recording, liens, HOA or condominium obligations, and dispute provisions. Use `financial-advisor` separately for affordability, return, tax modeling, and financing comparisons.

## Present the review

Lead with what the document does and the few provisions that matter most. Separate:

- acceptable or ordinary terms
- negotiable business choices
- legal uncertainties requiring jurisdiction-specific verification
- missing material
- high-consequence provisions worth professional review

When suggesting language, explain the intended effect and assumptions. Do not imply that inserting a clause guarantees enforceability or completeness.
