# Disputes and Procedure

Use this reference for demands, threatened or active disputes, service of process, court or agency proceedings, deadlines, evidence preservation, negotiation, mediation, arbitration, and settlement.

## Triage first

Before exploring the merits, identify any immediate need to:

- protect physical safety or comply with an emergency order
- avoid questioning, signing, payment, transfer, waiver, or destruction that could materially prejudice the user
- preserve a response, appeal, hearing, claim, cure, notice, or limitations deadline
- notify an insurer, employer, agency, landlord, lender, or other required recipient
- preserve documents, messages, recordings, physical items, electronically stored information, and metadata
- obtain qualified representation because liberty, immigration status, custody, housing, major assets, or irreversible rights are at risk

Do not assume a deadline from memory. Read the document, identify the service or trigger date, and verify the governing rule and counting method.

## Build a neutral record

Create a chronology that distinguishes:

- events personally observed by the user
- statements made by other people
- documents or records supporting an event
- assumptions, disputes, and missing evidence
- communications already sent and positions already taken

Preserve originals. Do not advise editing, deleting, backdating, concealing, or selectively destroying material. Avoid turning unverified accusations into factual declarations.

## Analyze the dispute

Identify:

- the forum, parties, standing, jurisdiction, venue, and procedural posture
- each plausible claim, element, burden, defense, exception, and remedy
- contractual notice, cure, escalation, mediation, arbitration, or exhaustion requirements
- damages, mitigation, causation, collectability, insurance, fees, and nonfinancial consequences
- the strongest contrary account and the fact most likely to determine the outcome

Separate legal merit from practical leverage. A technically valid claim may be expensive or difficult to prove or collect; a weak legal claim may still create settlement pressure. Explain both without guaranteeing an outcome.

## Compare resolution paths

Use principled negotiation: interests, objective criteria, options, BATNA, reservation point, and the cost of no agreement. Compare informal resolution, demand, internal appeal, agency complaint, mediation, arbitration, small claims, ordinary litigation, and professional representation only when actually available.

Before suggesting a communication, consider whether it could create an admission, waive a right, trigger a deadline, reveal strategy, violate an order, contact a represented person improperly, or undermine insurance coverage. Draft in the user's voice, keep factual assertions supportable, and label settlement communications accurately without assuming a label makes them privileged or inadmissible.

## Court and agency submissions

Prefer current official forms and instructions. Verify:

- the correct tribunal, case number, parties, caption, and requested relief
- standing orders, local rules, formatting, page limits, exhibits, redaction, privacy, signature, service, fees, and filing method
- that every cited authority and quotation exists, is current, supports the proposition, and applies to the jurisdiction and posture
- that each factual statement is true, supported by the record, and consistent with declarations and exhibits

Do not file, serve, sign, or contact anyone unless the user separately requests that external action and provides the required authorization. Make clear that the signer remains responsible for the submission.

## Escalate usefully

Recommend prompt qualified help when a deadline is near, interests are adverse, the matter affects liberty or immigration status, physical safety is at risk, custody or housing may change, the value or irreversibility is substantial, or the procedure exceeds reliable self-help. State the relevant specialty and help the user prepare a concise chronology, document list, objectives, questions, and deadline summary rather than merely saying "get a lawyer."
